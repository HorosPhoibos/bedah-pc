Procurement

Buying and renting out prebuilt PCs for companies

Prebuilt (branded) PCs are assembled at the factory: Dell, HP, Lenovo, Asus, Acer, and local brands. When procuring dozens to hundreds of units, companies almost always choose these over custom builds.

Why companies choose prebuilt

  • One warranty covers the whole unit, often on-site (a technician comes to you).
  • Every unit is identical, so mass setup and maintenance are easy.
  • An OEM Windows licence is already installed and legal.
  • Remote management features (Intel vPro / AMD PRO) and TPM security.

Common business lines

  • Dell Pro (formerly OptiPlex)
  • HP ProDesk / EliteDesk
  • Lenovo ThinkCentre
  • Asus ExpertCenter, Acer Veriton
  • Local brands with TKDN such as Axioo, Advan, Zyrex

Business lines differ from consumer lines: longer production runs, better warranties, and more stable components.

Form factors

  • Tower: easiest to upgrade, can take a GPU.
  • SFF (small form factor): slim, with a few slots.
  • Tiny / Mini PC: book-sized, can mount behind a monitor. Saves space, good for admin staff.
  • All-in-One: the PC is built into the monitor. Tidy, but if the screen breaks the whole unit goes in for service.

Government and state-owned procurement

Indonesian government agencies buy through the LKPP e-Katalog. Procurement rules (Presidential Regulation 16/2018 and its amendments) require domestic products where a product has a TKDN + BMP value of at least 40%.

That means the TKDN value in a unit's spec can decide whether a bid wins.

The case for renting

  • No big upfront capital; the cost becomes a predictable monthly operating expense.
  • Maintenance and replacement units for breakdowns are included.
  • Units are replaced with new ones every 3–4 years, with no hassle selling the old ones.
  • Suits temporary projects, events, or contract staff.

Reasonable lifespan

Office PCs are usually replaced every 3–5 years. Laptops every 3–4 years because the battery degrades. Older units usually still turn on, but they slow down, their warranty runs out, and they may not be supported by the latest Windows.

Quality control

Checklist before a unit goes to a client

Units that pass QC rarely get complaints. Do this for every rental or sale unit, and keep the results on file.

  1. Physical check and serial numberNo dents in the case, all screws present, no damaged ports. Photograph the unit and record the serial number for the asset tag.
  2. Enter the BIOSThe CPU, RAM capacity, and SSD are detected as specified. Date and time are correct. XMP/EXPO enabled if needed. Update the BIOS if there's a newer stable version.
  3. Storage healthCrystalDiskInfo must show "Good". Record the remaining life (health %) and power-on hours for used units.
  4. Stress test and temperaturesCPU for 15 minutes and GPU for 10 minutes (OCCT or Cinebench). No shutdowns or errors, and the CPU shouldn't sit above about 90 °C.
  5. RAM testWindows Memory Diagnostic or MemTest86 for at least one pass on used units or after changing RAM.
  6. Test every portEach USB, audio, LAN, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, monitor port, and webcam. For laptops also the keyboard, touchpad, screen (dead pixels), and battery.
  7. SoftwareWindows activated and updated, all drivers installed (no warning marks in Device Manager), the client's standard apps installed.
  8. Clean and packDust, fingerprints, and old stickers. Asset label, all cables, and a signed handover document.
Trading

Checking a used unit before buying

Identity and legitimacy

  • Match the serial number on the sticker with the one shown in the BIOS.
  • Check the warranty status on the manufacturer's official site (Dell, HP, Lenovo) using the serial number. The site also shows the original factory specs.
  • Make sure there's no BIOS password, company account (Intune/Autopilot), or active anti-theft lock. Units like that can lock up after a reset.

Hardware health

  • SSD/HDD: health % and power-on hours in CrystalDiskInfo.
  • Laptop battery: type powercfg /batteryreport in Command Prompt, then compare "Design capacity" with "Full charge capacity". Below 70%, factor in a battery replacement.
  • A short stress test and a temperature check.
  • Screen: dead pixels, shadows, and laptop hinges.

Accessories and licences

  • An original charger with the right wattage.
  • Windows: a digital licence or a COA sticker. Many business PCs carry a Windows Pro licence embedded in the BIOS.
  • If the RAM or SSD has been replaced, note the brand and capacity.
Client data

Wiping data safely when a unit comes back

Indonesia has Law No. 27 of 2022 on Personal Data Protection. Rental units returning from clients can hold data about their staff and customers. Wiping it properly is a responsibility, and also a selling point.

Why "format" isn't enough

A normal delete or a quick format only removes the index. The actual files can still be recovered with free recovery software.

SSDs

  • Use the Secure Erase feature in the SSD maker's app (Samsung Magician, Kingston SSD Manager, etc.), or the data-wipe feature in a business PC's BIOS (Dell Data Wipe, HP Secure Erase, Lenovo ThinkShield).
  • Alternative: Windows "Reset this PC" → "Remove everything" → turn on "Clean data".
  • Overwriting tools made for HDDs aren't suitable for SSDs.

HDDs

  • Use a wiping program that overwrites the whole disk, or Reset this PC with "Clean data".
  • Failed HDDs that can't be wiped are physically destroyed.

Document it

  • Record the serial number, date, method, and technician's name.
  • Give the client a data-erasure certificate.
  • Remove the client's Microsoft account, email accounts, and the device from their "Find my device" list before renting it out again.
Client cheat sheet

Questions you'll almost certainly be asked

Short answers you can give straight away in a presentation or when a client asks.

"What spec is enough for admin staff?"

A current-generation Core i3 / Core Ultra 5 / Ryzen 5, 16 GB of RAM (2×8), a 512 GB NVMe SSD, Windows 11 Pro. No graphics card needed. A spec like this handles Office, a browser with lots of tabs, Zoom, and accounting software comfortably for 4–5 years.

"Why an SSD? HDDs are bigger and cheaper."

An SSD starts the PC in a dozen seconds or so and opens apps instantly. An HDD can take 1–2 minutes just to boot. For staff, that waiting time is a cost. HDDs are fine for archives or backups.

"Is our Windows 10 still safe to use?"

Official Windows 10 support ended in October 2025. Microsoft extended security updates through the ESU programme until 12 October 2027, but only for enrolled devices (paid per year for businesses). After that, PCs get no security patches. Plan the move to Windows 11, which needs TPM 2.0, Secure Boot, and an 8th-gen Intel or Ryzen 2000 CPU or newer. PCs older than that should be replaced.

"Why is it more expensive than last year?"

Since late 2025, memory chip makers (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) have shifted production to memory for AI data centres. RAM and SSD prices have multiplied and pushed up PC and laptop prices. This is happening worldwide, not just at one shop.

"What's the difference between Core i5 and Core Ultra 5?"

Core Ultra is Intel's new naming since late 2023. The 5 / 7 / 9 still shows the class. Core Ultra chips are generally more power-efficient and have an NPU (a dedicated AI chip). What matters more than the name is the generation. A Core Ultra 5 225 is newer than a Core i5-12400.

"What does our design / architecture team need?"

A class-7 CPU (Core Ultra 7 / Ryzen 7), 32 GB of RAM, a 1 TB NVMe SSD, and a graphics card such as an RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB or better. For apps like AutoCAD, Revit, SketchUp + rendering, or Premiere, the GPU and RAM matter most. A colour-accurate monitor matters too.

"Desktops or laptops for staff?"

Desktops: cheaper for the same power, more durable, easier to repair, and harder to lose. Laptops: for staff who work out of the office or in meetings. Many companies mix them: desktops for desk staff, laptops for sales and management.

"What's an AI PC / Copilot+ PC? Do we need one?"

A Copilot+ PC is a laptop with an NPU of at least 40 TOPS (for example Snapdragon X, Ryzen AI 300, Core Ultra 200V, Core Ultra Series 3). The NPU runs Windows AI features locally and saves battery. For ordinary office work it isn't required yet. The web version of Copilot still runs on normal PCs.

"Why does our 1 TB SSD only show 931 GB?"

Manufacturers count in decimal (1 TB = 1,000 GB), Windows counts in binary. The capacity is all there; nothing is missing.

"Why does the PC suddenly shut off during heavy work?"

The three most common causes: overheating (dust or a cooler problem), a PSU that's too weak or worn out, or an unstable wall socket or UPS. Check temperatures with HWiNFO, then try another PSU.
