Summary

Laptop vs PC, seen from the inside

The components do exactly the same jobs as in a PC. The difference is that everything is packed onto one thin board, shares one small cooler, and much of it is permanently soldered.

ComponentIn a PCIn a laptop
CPUIn a socket, replaceableAlmost always soldered to the mainboard, not replaceable
GPUA separate card in a PCIe slotA chip soldered to the mainboard, sharing heatpipes with the CPU
RAMLong DIMMs, 2–4 slotsShort SO-DIMMs (0–2 slots), or permanently soldered in thin laptops
StorageM.2, SATA, 3.5" HDDM.2 (sometimes the short 2230), older laptops: 2.5" HDD/SSD
CoolingLarge heatsinks, several fansThin heatpipes and 1–2 small blower fans
PowerPSU inside the caseAn external adapter + an internal battery
Wi-FiOften built into the motherboard, or a PCIe cardA small M.2 card with antenna cables to the screen

Before opening a laptop

  • Fully shut it down (not sleep) and unplug the charger.
  • Remove the bottom panel, then unplug the battery connector first before touching anything else.
  • Laptop screws are often different lengths. Note or photograph where each goes.
  • Use a plastic tool (spudger) to release clips, not a metal screwdriver.

The upgrades that make sense

  • HDD to SSD: the most noticeable for older laptops.
  • Add RAM: only if there's still a free SO-DIMM slot.
  • Replace the battery: if capacity is below about 70% or it's swollen.
  • Clean the fan and replace the thermal paste: hot, noisy laptops are often cured by this.

Check before renting out or selling

  • Battery health: powercfg /batteryreport.
  • Hinges, screen (dead pixels), keyboard, touchpad, webcam, every port.
  • Temperatures during a stress test and fan noise.
  • An original charger with the right wattage.
