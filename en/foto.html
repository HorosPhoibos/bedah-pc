Summary

Custom-built vs prebuilt, seen from the inside

Both have a CPU, RAM, motherboard, and storage. What differs is how the factory designed them.

What you seeCustom-built PCPrebuilt PC (Dell, HP, Lenovo)
MotherboardStandard sizes (ATX, mATX), can be bought and replaced from any brandA factory-specific design, often only replaceable with a board from the same model
Power connectorsStandard ATX 24-pin + CPU 8-pinOften factory-specific (for example Dell's 8-pin), so the PSU must be the same model
HeatsinksLarge and plentiful, for powerful CPUs and GPUsMinimal, because office CPUs are low-power and helped by air ducts
PortsLots of USB, gaming ports, full audioLegacy office ports: VGA, serial, PS/2
ServicingNeeds a screwdriver, cables routed however you likeTool-less blue latches, cables and positions set by the factory
UpgradesAlmost everything can be replacedRAM and SSD are easy. The GPU, PSU, and cooler are limited by case size and special connectors

The photos below show units a few generations old, because freely licensed photos of the newest models are still scarce. The layout is the same as today's PCs: the socket, RAM slots, M.2, PCIe, and power connectors are all in the same places.
