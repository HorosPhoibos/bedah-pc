Cables

What's inside a cable, and why prices vary so much

Two cables that look the same can differ in price by 5×. The difference is inside: the metal, the thickness, the shielding, and the certification.

Inside a cable

  • Conductor: the metal wire that carries power or signal, ideally pure copper.
  • Insulation: plastic around each wire so they don't short.
  • Shielding: a foil or braided metal layer that blocks outside interference. Important in data cables.
  • Jacket: the outer skin that protects against bending and abrasion.
  • Connector: the plug. How well it's crimped decides how long the cable lasts.

Pure copper vs CCA

Cheap cables often use CCA (Copper Clad Aluminium): aluminium with a thin copper coating. It's more resistive, heats up more easily, and breaks more easily.

CCA LAN cables can make connections unstable over long runs and aren't suitable for PoE (CCTV or access points powered over the LAN cable). For office installations, ask for pure copper.

Wire thickness (AWG)

A smaller AWG number means a thicker wire. Quality PSU power cables use 16–18 AWG. Wire that's too thin for a large current can heat up and even melt.

That's why the 12V-2x6 GPU connector has to be plugged in all the way and must not be bent sharply near the plug.

Expensive isn't always better

HDMI, DisplayPort, and USB signals are digital, so there are only two outcomes: it works or it doesn't. A certified Rp150K HDMI cable gives exactly the same picture as a Rp1.5M one.

Gold-plated connectors only help against corrosion. Pay for certification and version, not for the brand or the shine.

Laptops

Same components, different arrangement

A laptop has the same CPU, RAM, SSD, and GPU doing the same jobs. The difference is that everything is packed onto one thin board and shares a small cooler.

Laptop processors

Look at the letters after the name. U is battery-friendly for office work, H / HX are powerful for gaming and editing, V / Lunar Lake are thin and efficient. Examples: Core Ultra 7 255H, Ryzen AI 7 350.

Laptop CPUs are soldered to the board, so they can't be replaced.

RAM is often soldered

Many thin laptops use LPDDR5X RAM that's permanently attached. That means the capacity you buy is the capacity forever. Laptops that still have SO-DIMM slots can be upgraded.

Advice: if the RAM is soldered, get at least 16 GB from the start.

Laptop GPUs are weaker

A laptop RTX 5070 isn't as strong as a desktop RTX 5070, because it's limited by power and heat. Look for the TGP (watts) in the specs: higher means faster.

Only in laptops

  • Battery (Wh): bigger lasts longer.
  • Built-in screen: check resolution, refresh rate, and brightness (nits).
  • A power adapter replaces the PSU.
  • Keyboard, touchpad, webcam, and Wi-Fi are built in.
Software

The layers on top of the hardware

Hardware can't do anything without software. Each layer talks to the layer below it. Tap a layer to see its explanation.

What happens when you press the power button

  1. Power comes inThe PSU turns on all its voltages and the motherboard signals "power good".
  2. UEFI firmware runsA small program on the motherboard chip wakes up before Windows does.
  3. Hardware check (POST)UEFI checks the CPU, RAM, and GPU. If the RAM has a problem, the PC beeps or a debug light turns on.
  4. Find a boot deviceUEFI looks for the SSD that holds the operating system, then runs the bootloader.
  5. OS loads into RAMThe Windows/Linux kernel is copied from the SSD into RAM, then drivers load.
  6. Login screenThe OS is ready. Apps you open are copied from the SSD into RAM, then run by the CPU.
Deep dive

How a computer really works

This section is the core of a Computer Organisation and Architecture course, condensed. Once this makes sense, you can explain why a spec makes sense instead of just memorising numbers.

1. The memory hierarchy: why there's cache, RAM, and an SSD

The closer to the CPU, the faster but also the smaller and more expensive. To make it relatable, let's slow time down: pretend 1 nanosecond is 1 second.

These are typical textbook orders of magnitude, not measurements of one product. The bar scale is logarithmic: each step to the right is 10× slower.

This is why full RAM makes a PC feel like it's "hanging". Windows has to move data onto the SSD (the page file), which is hundreds of times slower than RAM. It's also why an HDD makes Windows slow: every small file needs a physical head to move into position.

2. The CPU's work cycle

  1. FetchThe CPU fetches the next instruction from memory (usually already in the cache).
  2. DecodeThe instruction is decoded: is it an addition, a comparison, or a command to load data?
  3. ExecuteThe arithmetic unit (ALU) or the floating-point unit (FPU) carries it out.
  4. Write backThe result is stored in a register or memory, and the cycle repeats billions of times per second.

Modern CPUs overlap many instructions at once (pipelining), guess which way a program will branch before it happens (branch prediction), and have several cores, each running this cycle on its own. That's why GHz alone isn't enough to compare CPUs from different generations. What decides it is IPC (instructions per clock) × GHz × the number of cores the application actually uses.

3. Transistors and "nanometres"

A CPU contains tens of billions of transistors, microscopic switches that are 0 or 1. The manufacturing processes have names like 4 nm, 3 nm, or Intel 18A.

Those numbers are now just generation names for the factory process, not the real size of a transistor. Examples: Ryzen 9000 is made on TSMC N4P, Core Ultra 200S uses TSMC N3B, and Core Ultra Series 3 laptops (Panther Lake) use Intel 18A. Newer processes are generally more power-efficient.

4. PCIe lanes

Fast components talk over PCIe lanes. One lane is roughly:

  • Gen3 ≈ 1 GB/s
  • Gen4 ≈ 2 GB/s
  • Gen5 ≈ 4 GB/s

A GPU uses 16 lanes (x16), an NVMe SSD uses 4 lanes (x4). So a Gen4 x4 NVMe tops out around 8 GB/s, which matches the ±7,000 MB/s on its box.

5. Bits vs bytes

1 byte = 8 bits. Internet speed is written in bits (Mbps), file sizes in bytes (MB).

100 Mbps internet means downloads of at most about 12.5 MB/s. This often makes clients feel their "internet isn't as advertised", even though the maths is right.

6. Why a 1 TB SSD shows 931 GB

Manufacturers count 1 TB = 1,000,000,000,000 bytes. Windows counts in powers of 2 (1 GB = 1,073,741,824 bytes). The result: 1 TB shows as about 931 GB.

No capacity is lost or stolen. Buyers ask about this very often.

7. Watts = Volts × Amps

Almost every PC component runs on the 12 V rail. A 360 W GPU pulls about 30 amps at 12 V. Current that large is why cable and connector quality matter.

8. Bottlenecks

A system is only as fast as its weakest part. A high-end GPU with an old CPU: the GPU waits for the CPU. A fast CPU with a single RAM stick: the CPU waits for data. A good build is balanced, not one super-expensive component.
